## Blind Spot 6: Presentation Order Vulnerability

The experiment results (03-results.md) show that reading order changes evaluator disposition. Map→Walk produces critics; Walk→Map produces observers. The same documents, different sequence, different scores.

This has an immediate practical implication for any sequential scoring system (including ScoreMyDeck):

**The order of information in a submission changes the score, regardless of the rubric.**

A deck that opens with a narrative, an image, or a micro-experience that activates the commutator sensorium will score differently than one that opens with a revenue model — even if all the same information is present. The evaluator is non-commutative. The rubric pretends to be commutative. This gap is exploitable.

### This Is Not a Bug. This Is How Persuasion Works.

The best pitch decks already do this intuitively. Great founders don't open with TAM slides. They open with a moment — a story, a demonstration, an image that shifts the room before the metrics arrive.

This is the commutator sensorium weaponized for persuasion: prime the felt-sense first, then present the formal case. The audience evaluates the formal case differently because the sensorium is already active.

### Defense: Commutator-Aware Scoring

The only defense against presentation-order effects is to **break the sequential evaluation**:

- Score each axis with a clean-context subagent that sees only the information relevant to that axis
- Randomize the order of axis evaluation
- Aggregate scores post-hoc
- Compare against a sequential-read baseline to measure priming effects

This is our four-subagent experimental design, turned into a scoring architecture. Marvin could offer this as a feature: "commutator-corrected scoring" that explicitly accounts for presentation order.

## The Reality Distortion Field Was Groove

Steve Jobs' famous "reality distortion field" is usually attributed to charisma, intensity, or force of personality. We propose a more precise explanation: **Jobs was grooving.**

Listen to any Jobs keynote. The cadence is wrong. Not wrong-random — wrong-structured. The pauses don't land where you expect. The emphasis falls off the grid. The sentence rhythm has micro-timing that's slightly, consistently, *dragged*.

This is the same mechanism as Dilla's micro-timing. The gap between expected cadence and actual cadence has shape. Your nervous system flags it: "something is coupled here, pay attention." The sensorium activates. And then, while your felt-sense detector is wide open, he tells you the iPhone will change the world — and you believe him.

### The Proof: Cargo Cult Cadence

Apple executives after Jobs — Tim Cook, Craig Federighi, others — reproduce the form of his presentation style. The pauses. The "one more thing." The measured pacing. But it lands flat. Why?

Because they put the pauses *where the pauses are supposed to go*. They're quantized. They're playing the right notes at the right time on the grid. Jobs played them *off* the grid — structured, not random, but non-commutative with expectation.

The cargo cult executives demonstrate G = 0 by attempting to reproduce G ≠ 0 through imitation. Same components, "correct" order, no groove. This is the commutator made audible: the difference between reality distortion and corporate keynote is literally the difference between non-zero and zero G.

### Implications for Scoring

If Jobs submitted a deck to ScoreMyDeck, the deck's score would depend on whether Marvin "heard" the groove. A text-based scoring system processes the content but not the cadence. It would score Jobs' deck the same as Tim Cook's deck if the words were identical.

This suggests another missing axis: **Presentation Groove** — does the submission itself have non-commutative cadence? Not just "is the content about non-commutativity" (Generative Surface Area) but "does the *form* of the submission embody it?"

This is hard to measure. It might be impossible to measure. Which is, again, the point.
